Learn this word for word · 45 seconds
“Moodly is a desktop app that slowly steers your mood through your music. Every Spotify song is a point in a 2D mood space: valence (how positive it sounds) against energy. Your current mood is the average of what you're listening to. You pick a target mood (Focused, Calm, Energized or Wind Down). A step planner over that space picks the next small step toward the target. Moodly then finds songs at that step that also fit musically and match your taste and languages, and puts them into a private session playlist that plays without a gap. It also slowly changes your screen warmth. I built it spec-first with Claude Code: a written SPEC, a reviewed PLAN, tests for every acceptance criterion, and a change log for every modification.”

If they ask “why does it matter?”: it applies the iso principle from music therapy. You start where the listener is and change the music gradually, not with a sudden jump.

How the parts connect

Mood space and the step planner

backend/mood_space.py · selection.py

What: Mood is a point (valence, energy) in the unit square [0,1]². Valence and energy come from each song's audio features. The square is cut into a 10 × 10 grid. Each cell is a state. From any cell, the planner may only move to a neighbouring cell that is strictly closer to the target. The target cell is absorbing (probability 1 of staying).

P[i → j] ∝ exp(−β · d(cⱼ, target)) pull toward the target (β = 6) × exp(−γ · d(cᵢ, cⱼ)²) penalty for big jumps (γ = 20) × w_skip(direction) learned from your skips w_skip = 2(1 + completions) / (2 + completions + skips) ← Beta(1,1) prior, unseen = 1.0

Three points you must not mix up:

  • Mood m: the average of the last 3 songs you heard (not skipped), the song playing, and your own songs ahead (up to 20). Moodly's picks only count once they play.
  • Heading h: the average of the whole upcoming window, Moodly's picks included.
  • Waypoint w: one planner step from h toward the target t. New songs are searched near w.

Why a step planner? The spec forbids simple genre swapping. A probability-based planner gives small, monotone steps that never move away from the target. β and γ are easy to tune, and the skip weight lets your behaviour reshape the path.

Why is the current mood an average of songs, not one song?

One song is noisy. A shuffle can throw in one sad song between happy ones. Averaging the recent and upcoming window gives a stable estimate. Moodly's own picks are left out until they actually play, so the app can't “cheat” by queueing songs and calling that progress.

What do β and γ do? What happens if you make them bigger?

β is the pull toward the target. Bigger β means the planner prefers the neighbour closest to the target, so steps become almost deterministic. γ penalises long jumps (a diagonal step is longer than a straight one). Bigger γ means more straight, small steps and smoother transitions.

Why only neighbours that are strictly closer to the target?

It guarantees progress. Every step reduces the distance, so the planner can never go backwards or loop. It always ends in the target cell, which is absorbing. On a grid every non-target cell has at least one closer neighbour, so no state is a dead end.

How do skips change the planner?

For each of the 8 directions I count completions and skips of songs that moved in that direction. The weight is the Beta posterior mean 2(1+c)/(2+c+s). With no data it is 1, so nothing changes. If you keep skipping songs in a direction, that direction's probability falls.

Why separate the “mood” and the “heading”?

The mood is what you've actually heard. The heading is where the whole queue is going, including songs Moodly already added. If I only used the mood, every cycle would aim at the same step and add the same kind of song again. Using the heading makes each new pick go one step further, like a staircase.

Picking, placing and removing songs

backend/selection.py (pure functions)

What: Each cycle (on a song change, a target change, or every ~90 s), candidates must be closer to the target than both the mood and the heading. They are searched within a radius of the waypoint, then ranked by a score where lower is better:

score = distance to waypoint + w_tempo·tempo gap (half/double time counts as close) + w_key·key gap (Camelot wheel) + w_loudness·loudness gap + w_feel·(danceability, acousticness gap) − 0.12·taste − 0.03·freshness

Then MMR (Maximal Marginal Relevance, λ = 0.7) chooses a varied set: no near-duplicates, no repeated artist. Songs go in at random positions in the middle of the queue (never now playing or next). The only removal rule: drop upcoming songs far from both the mood and the target (radius 0.35), with a cap per cycle (up to 5 right after a new target).

Why “musical fit”? A song can be at the right mood point and still feel jarring (a 70 BPM ballad after a 140 BPM track, a clashing key). Tempo, Camelot key and loudness keep transitions smooth, like a DJ would.

What is MMR and why use it?

Maximal Marginal Relevance picks items one at a time. Each time it balances how good a song is (relevance) against how similar it is to songs already picked. λ = 0.7 means 70% relevance, 30% diversity. Without it you'd get five near-identical songs from the same artist.

What is the Camelot wheel?

A DJ's map of the 24 musical keys arranged in a circle. Neighbours on the wheel (same number, or ±1) mix harmonically. I measure key distance as steps on that wheel, so compatible keys score as close.

Why random insertion positions, if “path” order converges faster?

It's a trade-off I chose on purpose. Random positions mean you hear new songs soon. In simulation the path order converges faster (0.59 → 0.04 in 3 h, against 0.59 → 0.19 in 4 h for random). Both modes exist in config (session.insert_mode). The path mode inserts at the gap with the cheapest cost T(a,c) + T(c,b) − T(a,b).

Why must a pick be closer to the target than the mood?

The mood is an average. Adding a song further from the target than the average would pull the average away. Only songs strictly closer can move it the right way.

Which songs are never removed?

The song playing, the next one, podcasts, and songs you added yourself with Spotify's “Add to queue”. Songs are matched by title + artists, because Spotify gives the same song different IDs in different countries (relinking).

The session playlist trick

backend/actuators/session.py

The problem: Spotify's Web API can only append to the play queue. It can't insert at a position or remove anything.

The fix: at Start session, Moodly creates a private playlist “Ambient Mood session” from the song playing and your queue, then plays it from the same song at the same millisecond. Playlists can be edited at any position. At the end, only the current song keeps playing, and the playlist is emptied and deleted.

A bug found only in live tests: Spotify plays a snapshot of the playlist, so edits didn't show up. Fix: after each change, replay the playlist from the same song and moment. Later bug: replaying “at position N” cut songs after a second, because Spotify's count was ahead of the API. Fix: name the song itself, not its position (M27).

Why not just add songs to the user's queue?

That was Phase 1. But append-only means you can't place songs in between or remove songs that pull the mood the wrong way. The playlist gives full control, while the music never stops.

What if the user starts other music during a session?

Moodly stops managing and says so in the sidebar. It doesn't fight the user and never pauses playback. There's a 20-second grace period after hand-over, and one odd reading isn't treated as a takeover. That fixed a bug where sessions ended a second after starting (M9).

What happens if the app crashes mid-session?

The leftover playlist is found and deleted on the next start. The tray app also owns the daemon and stops it cleanly, and the installer asks the app to quit before uninstalling.

Inference: polling, skips, audio features

backend/inference.py · features.py

What: the daemon polls Spotify every 0.75 s while music plays (2 s when paused), with back-off on HTTP 429. When the song changes, it estimates how far the old song got. Under 80% of its length counts as a skip.

Big obstacle: Spotify turned off /audio-features for new apps in November 2024 (it returns 403). So features come from a fallback chain: Spotify → ReccoBeats → local CSV dataset → a 30-second iTunes preview analysed by ReccoBeats. Every result is cached in SQLite.

How do you detect a skip if Spotify doesn't tell you?

A heuristic. When the track ID changes, the old song's progress = its last seen progress + time since that poll (clamped to the gap between the two polls). If that's under 80% of its duration, it's a skip.

What design pattern is the feature chain?

Chain of responsibility with caching. Each provider has the same interface. If one has no answer, the next is tried. Nothing is looked up twice.

What are valence and energy?

Spotify-style audio features from 0 to 1. Valence = how positive or happy the music sounds. Energy = intensity and activity. Together they match Russell's circumplex model of emotion (pleasure vs arousal), which is why I used them as the two axes.

Recommender: taste + top-2 languages

recommender/ (Phase 3)

What: builds a live song pool per session. Taste comes from your Spotify top tracks (4 weeks and 6 months, weighted by rank) plus plays and skips in Moodly. Candidates are songs by your favourite artists and their Deezer related artists, including the last two years' releases. Only songs in your top-2 languages are kept (second language only if ≥ 15% share). A session adds nothing until the pool has 15 useful songs (max 10 minutes).

Why? The first version picked from a 2022 dataset by mood only. You got random songs in random languages. For an Indian listener (Telugu 52%, Hindi 33% in my live test) that felt wrong.

Spotify gives no language. How do you find a song's language?

In order of trust: (1) a marker in the title or album like [TELUGU], (2) native script in the title, album or artist, (3) the script of the lyrics from LRCLIB, (4) common words in romanized lyrics, (5) the artist's usual language (≥ 2 known songs, ≥ 70% one language). Otherwise unknown and not used.

Why does the marker beat the lyrics?

Dubbed film songs. KGF 2's Telugu version returned Tamil lyrics from LRCLIB. The title marker [TELUGU] is more reliable, so it wins. That case is a test.

Why wait for 15 songs before adding anything?

Before this, when the pool was empty, random catalog songs in any language got added. Waiting until enough good songs exist is better. To keep music going, if fewer than 2 songs are left ahead, it still adds (pool, then your songs, then the catalog).

Two processes talking through files

shared/config_io.py

What: the UI writes the target mood and session flags to config.json. The daemon's file watcher sees it within ~50 ms. The daemon writes the live mood, song and queue to state.json, and the UI watches that. Writes are atomic: write a temp file, then os.replace.

Why files and not sockets? The spec asked for it. It's simple, easy to debug (you can open the file), and if the UI crashes the daemon keeps going. Atomic replace means a reader never sees half a file.

What would break without the atomic write?

The reader could open the file while it's half written and get invalid JSON, or wrong values. os.replace swaps the whole file in one step.

How did you fix the sidebar lag?

(M17, M28) Faster polling while playing, re-average the mood right away on a song change, write state.json immediately when something visible changes, and run the slow session steps in a background thread so polling never waits. The spec target is under 1 second.

The tray sidebar

frontend/ (PyQt6)

What: a tray icon opens a frameless glass sidebar: now playing, a small mood map, the four mood rows, Start/End/New session, and the account. The glass is tinted by the current mood: amber = happy and energetic, rose = tense, violet = sad, teal = calm.

Why PyQt6 and not Tkinter?

Native tray icon (QSystemTrayIcon), frameless transparent windows, and pytest-qt so UI clicks are tested automatically (each click must update config.json in under 100 ms).

How is the blur done?

When the sidebar opens it takes a snapshot of the screen behind it and blurs that. Windows' Acrylic API painted black behind Qt's transparent windows, so I dropped it. Downside: a moving video behind it isn't updated until you reopen.

Accounts and security

backend/spotify/auth.py

What: sign-in uses OAuth Authorization Code + PKCE. The app ships no client secret, only the public Client ID. Tokens are stored only on the user's computer, and only for Premium accounts (Spotify only lets Premium accounts control playback).

What is PKCE and why use it?

The app makes a random code verifier and sends only its SHA-256 hash (code challenge, S256) in the sign-in URL. When it swaps the code for a token, it sends the verifier. Only the app that started the sign-in can finish it. A desktop app can't keep a secret safe, so PKCE removes the need for one.

Can anyone download it and use it?

Not yet. The Spotify app is in Development mode: only 5 allow-listed users, and the owner needs Premium. Opening it to everyone needs Spotify's extended quota, which is for organizations. This is the biggest limit and it's Spotify policy, not code.

Screen warmth

backend/actuators/display.py

What: each mood has a screen colour temperature in Kelvin (Energized 6500 K → Wind Down 3500 K). The screen moves 100 K per minute using Win32 SetDeviceGammaRamp.

Why can't Wind Down reach 3500 K on some PCs?

Windows rejects gamma ramps warmer than about 3400–4000 K by default. The app detects that and stops at the warmest accepted value. A registry setting unlocks the full range (in the README).

Feedback log

backend/feedback.py

What: every song change is logged to SQLite: time, mood vector, intervention type, skipped or not. Skip rates of Moodly's songs vs normal songs show if the interventions annoy you, and they feed the planner's skip weights and the taste score.

Spotify's 2026 API changes

backend/spotify/client.py
  • /playlists/{id}/tracks became /items, and spotipy still uses the old path, so I call the new endpoints directly.
  • /me may drop the product field. Fallback: a 403 PREMIUM_REQUIRED on the first playback command means not Premium.
  • No artist genres, no popularity, max 10 search results per page. That's why languages come from lyrics and markers.

Weak spots they may push on

Say these yourself before they find them. Admitting a limit and giving the next step sounds strong.

Does it actually measure the user's mood?

No. It measures the mood of the music you choose, as a proxy. It assumes what you pick reflects how you feel. Real validation would need self-reports (e.g. a quick mood rating before and after) or a user study. That's the honest next step.

It's slow. Why does a mood change take so long?

The mood is an average of about 20 songs, so it only moves as songs play or are removed. A full change can take hours in simulation. Faster options: a smaller window, more removals per cycle, or a recency-weighted average. Slow is partly the point (gradual, iso principle).

What doesn't work yet?

5-user Spotify limit; about 25% of songs have unknown language; the Mac build is untested on a real Mac and has no screen warmth; podcasts are ignored; the session playlist is visible in your library; free APIs (Deezer, LRCLIB, iTunes, ReccoBeats) may rate-limit or disappear (all cached, failures skipped).
